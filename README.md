# resorts world las vegas: which hotel to pick, what the $55 resort fee really adds, and how to get cashback on the bill

Most people searching for Resorts World Las Vegas aren't deciding whether to go to Vegas. They've already decided. They're trying to work out which of the three hotels inside the building they should actually book, what the final nightly number looks like once fees land on it, and whether the north end of the Strip is worth the walk.

That last question matters more than it sounds, because Resorts World isn't one hotel. It's three, sharing a casino floor, a pool deck and a very long corridor. Pick wrong and you can end up paying Conrad money for a Hilton room, or Crockfords money for a view of the convention center roof [1].

Here's what the property actually is, what it costs, and how to book it without leaving a few hundred dollars on the table.

## Three hotels, one address

Resorts World opened on the north Strip in 2021, built and owned by Genting, and it operates as an integrated Hilton complex: [1][2]

- **Las Vegas Hilton at Resorts World** — 1,678 rooms, the value tier, the biggest room count of the three [1]
- **Conrad Las Vegas at Resorts World** — the largest Conrad property in the world; U.S. News lists 1,496 rooms, other booking sites show closer to 1,774, so treat the exact count as fuzzy and the scale as enormous [3][2]
- **Crockfords Las Vegas, LXR Hotels & Resorts** — 332 rooms, the boutique end, tucked behind its own lobby and its own casino floor [4]

All three sit at 111–999 Resorts World Avenue, roughly a five-minute drive from the Las Vegas Convention Center and Fashion Show Mall [1]. The complex includes about 250,000 square feet of conference space and 50 meeting rooms, which is why you'll see badge lanyards in the elevator on a Tuesday [1].

Practical consequence: the room you book determines your lobby, your pool access pattern and your walk to the casino. It does not determine your restaurant options. All 40-odd dining and bar outlets are open to all three hotels, under a "stay at one, eat, drink and play at three" arrangement [5].

## The $55 nobody puts in the headline

Every rate you see on a booking site is the room rate. The resort fee is separate, and at Resorts World it's **$55 per room per night**, which lands closer to **$62 once tax is added** [6][7].

That's not a rounding error on a three-night stay. It's about $186 on top of your quoted price, before parking.

Parking is its own line item: covered self-parking runs **$21 per day** and valet **$40 per day** with in-and-out privileges [7][8]. Nevada residents with NV plates park free, and Genting Rewards members at the Elite, Prime, Monarch and Imperial tiers get complimentary self-parking [6].

### The No Resort Fees offer

Resorts World runs an official "All Resort, No Fees" offer that waives the $55 nightly resort fee, valid for travel through December 28, 2026. You have to be 21 or older to check in and redeem it, and the waiver applies per night of the stay when the booking is made under that specific offer [6].

Two caveats worth knowing before you build a trip around it:

- The no-resort-fee nights are selective. A Hilton spokesperson told AwardWallet the properties will keep offering them "on select dates" in 2026, including on Conrad and Crockfords [9]. Finding them takes a bit of date-fiddling.
- If you book through Amex Fine Hotels + Resorts, the resort fee still applies on those reservations [9].

Also worth checking before you assume you're covered: some third-party listings and packages advertise "No Resort Fee" room categories directly, like the City View Premium Room — No Resort Fee or the Strip View Premium Room — No Resort Fee at Conrad [2]. Same outcome, different route.

## Which one actually fits your trip

**Las Vegas Hilton** is where you go if the room is a place to sleep and you'd rather spend the difference on dinner. The trade-off is real: reviewers describe the rooms as generic and the design as ordinary, with one guest advising you spend $50 more and stay elsewhere next time [13]. If you're out of the room from 9am to 2am, that math works.

**Conrad** is the middle option that's actually the sweet spot for most people. Lead-in rooms start at 550 square feet, which is genuinely large for the Strip, with floor-to-ceiling windows and a walk-in shower [11]. It's also the property most likely to have a no-resort-fee room category showing on your dates [2].

**Crockfords** is for people who want the quiet. 332 rooms means the elevator doesn't queue and the lobby bar isn't a scrum. It's also the one where a mediocre room would be most annoying at the price — at roughly $343 all-in for a sample night [4], you're paying for calm rather than features.

**Conrad Complete** deserves a specific note because Las Vegas all-inclusive packages usually show up at budget properties, not in the luxury segment. At $150 per guest per night on top of the room rate, it bundles a Club 66 lounge with breakfast and an evening social hour, a multi-course lunch, a multi-course dinner, reserved pool seating with beer and soft drinks, Zouk entry and valet [11]. A reviewer who stayed two nights on the plan reported nightly rates starting around $504 for a couple including the add-on and the resort fee, pre-tax [11].

Run that against à-la-carte prices: a sit-down lunch and dinner for two, plus valet at $40 and pool drinks, and the $150-per-person figure stops looking outrageous. It only makes sense if you're actually on property for meals rather than out at other casinos.

## What rooms really cost in practice

Ignore any single "average rate" you see. The spread is the story:

- A Conrad city-view Premier room has shown up from around **SGD 132** (≈ US$100) per night on a soft date [2]
- Conrad averages quoted on aggregator pages sit nearer **HKD 2,255** (≈ US$290) [12]
- Crockfords sample nights start around **US$343** including taxes and fees [4]

Currency conversions above are approximate and move daily; the point isn't the exact figure, it's that you can pay $100 or $350 for the same brand depending on when you go.

One more line item people miss: incidental deposit holds, which have been reported at **$150 per night** for the Hilton property [1].

If you're booking through an OTA, the price differences between platforms for the same room on the same dates are often wide enough to fund a dinner. ShopBack's own travel team puts the typical spread at $50–$200 per night for the same room across OTAs, which on a five-night stay is $250–$1,000 [14].

## What 8,000+ reviews keep saying

Conrad Las Vegas at Resorts World holds a **8.7/10** guest rating aggregated from 8,209 reviews, plus AAA Four Diamond and Forbes Travel Guide Recommended status for 2025 [3].

The complaints repeat with a pattern worth taking seriously:

- **Housekeeping detail.** Guests have reported empty shampoo dispensers and marks left in an uncleaned toilet in an otherwise upscale room [15]
- **The resort fee.** Listed as a downside in critic summaries despite the amenities it covers [3]
- **Restaurant pricing.** Guest reviews describe on-property dining as expensive; a trip.com reviewer noted the property is a "get what you pay for" situation and that expectations need managing at the lower end [12][16]
- **Checkout-day logistics.** One detailed trip report praised the rooms and the absence of resort fees, but flagged that after an 11am checkout with a 9pm flight there was nowhere on site to shower before heading to the airport [17]

The praise is consistent too: large, clean, quiet rooms; a well-organized casino floor; a strong dining roster; and the calmer atmosphere of the north Strip end compared to mid-Strip [16][17].

## Eating, drinking and what's on property

The scale is the thing. The resort lists around **40 restaurants and bars** across price points, from a food-hall style collection to Michelin-adjacent fine dining [5]. Names that come up repeatedly in reviews: Wally's Wine & Spirits, ¡VIVA! by Ray Garcia, Kusa Nori, FUHU, Brezza, Carversteak and Crossroads, the plant-based fine dining room [5][11][17].

Entertainment runs through the 5,000-capacity **Resorts World Theatre**, programmed by AEG Presents, with residencies that have included Carrie Underwood and Luke Bryan, and past runs from Katy Perry and Michael Bublé [18]. Zouk Nightclub and Ayu Dayclub handle the late-night side [18].

For a quieter evening, Allē Lounge on 66 sits on the 66th floor of Conrad with Strip views — multiple guests single it out as the best thing they did at the resort, particularly at sunset [17][19].

The pool deck is shared across the three hotels. Listings quote different counts, five or seven pools depending on how they're counted, plus a dayclub and separate adult and family areas [4][5]. If the pool is the reason you're booking, confirm what's open for your dates — pool operations change seasonally and by weekday.

**Is Resorts World worth it over a mid-Strip hotel?**

Conrad, for most trips. You get the space and finish of the luxury tier without Crockfords pricing, and it's the one most likely to show a fee-free room category [2][11].
